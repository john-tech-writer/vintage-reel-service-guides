## Vintage rod testing, restoration, and conservation

Initial cleaning and functional test: Rod field-tested with a Nobby baitcasting reel using a 1 oz lure. Casting performance was reported as approximately 75–100 feet, with no observed line snags, guide interference, or operational issue.

General surface cleaning: Entire rod—including blank, wraps, guides, cork grip, and butt cap—cleaned with a soft cosmetic-style brush using dilute Dawn dishwashing detergent in water. Rod was subsequently dried thoroughly. No adverse change to wraps, finish, or components was observed.

Butt cap treatment: Original dark hard-rubber butt cap dry-cleaned using fine steel wool. Chalking and surface oxidation were reduced while retaining original molded form, dark coloration, and age-related surface character. No dressing, oil, polish, dye, or coating applied.

Guide treatment status: Limited dry fine-steel-wool cleaning performed only on the interior line-contact surface of the first guide nearest the butt. No exterior polishing, liquid rust treatment, metal polish, guide removal, wrap disturbance, or cleaning of other guide exteriors undertaken. Further guide treatment, if any, will be limited to dry conservation methods after individual inspection and snag testing.

Clean enough to inspect and fish; preserve original parts, finish, wraps, markings, and age evidence; defer replacement or cosmetic refinishing unless a component becomes mechanically unsafe or line-damaging.

## Test casting

[ref the st. croix - this could be a good example, but genericize] Shakespeare places such short HD rods in the heavy class but treat 3/4 + oz. casting weight as a careful test range, not an assumption that it is a true heavy-power rod. A 4'6" fiberglass rod can feel stout in hand while still having a tip and ferrule-free (one-piece) blank that dislikes repeated hard acceleration with 1 oz. + weights.

Start progressively:

  - Cast a 1/2 oz. practice plug or sinker with a smooth lob.

  - Move to 3/4 oz. and watch the top third of the blank during the cast.

  - If it loads smoothly without the tip folding excessively or the butt feeling overstrained, try 1 oz—but avoid snap-casting.

  - Stop if you hear guide-wrap ticking, see stress whitening/cracks, or feel a sharp “hinge” in the blank.
