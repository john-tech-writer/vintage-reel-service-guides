# Cleaning and lubrication for vintage reels - YouTube script

Hello, and welcome to the Vintage Reel Service Guides. This video covers cleaning and lubricating reels after they've been disassembled.

Cleaning and lubricating are fairly generic procedures so the procedure outlined here can be applied to most mid-century American reels.

Cleaning

This reel has already been serviced, so it’s clean and lubricated, but here are some images of the gears before cleaning.

Generally the gears and inside of the case will have some accumulation of old grease, which hardens, prevents parts from moving, and no longer serves the purpose of lubricating the gears. There may also be grit, sand, and salt accumulation. First we clean, then we apply fresh grease.

Let's talk a little about tools and materials.

You'll need the following basic materials:

  - Some kind of solvent, here we are using paint thinner
  - Some kind of detergent, like this blue liquid
  - Oil made for fishing reels
  - Grease made for fishing reels

You'll also need the following basic tools, many of which can be salvaged from your house or workshop:

  - A small pan for washing the parts in solvent
  - A small brush
  - A dental pick or similar shaped instrument
  - Tweezers
  - Q-tips
  - Shop rags--old washcloths and socks are absorbent and work well
  - Screen for the sink drain so you won't lose small parts
  - A plastic dishpan

The cleaning process is basically 1-2-3:

First a bath in a solvent to dissolve the grease, then a bath in detergent and water to remove any remaining grease along with the solvent, then a thorough drying.

1. First place all the small parts in a small pan, then add a little solvent.

[put parts on pan, show solvent can]

Here we are using paint thinner. 1/4" on the bottom of the pan is plenty.
Using a small paintbrush work the solvent into the gear teeth. It may be necessary to let the parts soak for an hour or overnight--if so cover them to prevent evaporation.

[demo]

Brush solvent into the inside of the case and around parts like the eccentric gear.

[demo]

2. After all the old grease is dissolved or at least softened, take the small parts out and place them on a shop rag to drain for a minute or two.

[demo]

4. Next, place all the parts into a plastic dishpan and add a squirt of detergent, then add an inch or two of hot water to the pan.

[show detergent]

If you are working in a utility sink, make sure to place a screen in the drain to avoid accidentally losing small parts.

Brush the parts to remove the softened grease and grit, then rinse them in hot water. An old colander works for this.

Then lay the parts on a shop rag to dry thoroughly. A fan will help speed this process.

Lubricating

After the parts are all dry we can lubricate them. Use lubricants that are specifically recommened for fishing reels.

The basic principles of lubricating a reel are simple:

1. Don't use too much. A little coating of the surface is enough. More will attract sand and salt.

2. Gears get grease, like this:

[take main gear in hand and show how to apply grease and brush it in]

3. Everything else gets a drop or two of oil. This includes all screws, edges where plates fit together, and points where rotation occurs, like the base of the handle.

The package insert or schematic that came with the reel will usually have instructions on all the important lubrication points, and these documents can usually be located in one of the archives on the web, like ORCA. See the website for more information. If you can't locate the schematic, just follow these general rules and you'll be OK.

Now that everything is clean and lubricated we can reassemble and test the reel.
